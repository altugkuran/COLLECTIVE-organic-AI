# COLLECTIVE — Open Research Questions

## A. DNA-to-processor interface
- What is a plausible address/index representation and physical readout mechanism?
- How do read latency, throughput, error rates, durability, and random-access constraints compare with RAM and conventional storage?
- How could an instruction encoding, bootloader, and ISA be specified without confusing information storage with executable computation?
- What is the minimum credible demonstration of selective retrieval of a stored routine?

## B. Sensor, actuator, and manufacturing interfaces
- Which biochemical/optical/electrochemical sensors can provide calibrated data to an electronic controller?
- What feedback, transport, precursor supply, folding, and quality-control steps would be needed for processor-directed molecular production?
- Which components are experimentally demonstrated, which are extrapolations, and which are currently beyond reach?

## C. Collective learning
- Can heterogeneous units outperform fixed-rule and centralized baselines on unseen tasks?
- How should individual scores and collective rewards balance useful contribution, speed, accuracy, compute cost, and energy?
- Does the reward scheme induce wasteful computation or misleading credit allocation?
- Do network roles, specializations, or hierarchies emerge without being hard-coded?

## D. Emergent physical innovation
- Can units propose and test useful new functions without explicit goals to build structures, move, or replicate?
- How can experiments distinguish recombination of preloaded blueprints from novel design?
- What physical degrees of freedom must exist for an unanticipated capability to be possible at all?

## E. Safety and epistemology
- What independent containment and shutdown mechanisms are necessary before any physical experiments?
- How can delayed or unobserved harmful byproducts be detected?
- What observations would *falsify* the collective-intelligence hypothesis?
- What is the smallest useful negative result?

## Suggested first phase
Start with computational models and an expert literature/feasibility review. Do not infer wet-lab readiness from a successful simulation. Any biological work requires qualified institutional oversight, risk assessment, and applicable approvals.
